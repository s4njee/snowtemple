# Safety

Snow Temple inherits The Hero's Journey's checks, unchanged: the WCAG 2.3.1 flash and red-flash analyser, the regular-pattern analyser after the ITC (Ofcom) guidance, whole-field motion caps from optical flow, red-capped colour, the start gate and held frames. Their rules, sources and limits are written up in [The Hero's Journey's docs/safety.md](../../herosjourney/docs/safety.md). Every effect and preset here runs through them in `npm test`, at the default and at the strong dose (0.7).

What this family has found so far:

- **Snow flashes in its cells before it flashes as a frame.** The first `snowfall` measured `high` with the frame's mean luminance moving by 0.5%: big, bright flakes close to the viewer crossed the analyser's cells in a fraction of a second, and each crossing is a rise and a fall in that cell. Drawing near flakes out of focus, with their light spread over the blur circle, fixed it, because that is what near snow looks like. At high density the cells' flake counts still fluctuate enough to count, so `snowfall`'s density curve stops where the strong dose passes. The analyser does not check whether flashes in different cells happen together, which WCAG's "concurrently" asks; it errs towards counting.
- **Snow that falls as one sheet is a whole-field drift.** `hello`'s flakes all fall at about the same speed, and the motion check read 0.059 screen widths a second, over the 0.05 cap, until they were slowed. `snowfall`'s parallax breaks the fall into many speeds and reads 0.01. Box-shadow snow, which moves every flake together, is the extreme case.
- **The motion check cannot see sparse, faint snow.** Faint flakes on black leave only a few trackable blocks. A rigid fit to a dozen blocks gave spurious zooms (0.118 doublings a second) until the estimator required twenty blocks as evidence of whole-field motion. Driving into the snow left almost nothing trackable at all, so its expansion is held to the cap from the effect's own transform instead (docs/studies/vection.md).
