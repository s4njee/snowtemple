EXPERIMENT 01 / SNOW OVER THE PAGE

Firstsnow.

Thousands of flakes falling between you and the page, from a quarter of a metre to fifteen metres away. Each falls at its own terminal speed and drifts with gusts that sweep across the window. Far flakes shrink to bright points, and the few close to the lens are soft and wide.

HOW THE SNOW FALLS

No two
flakes alike.

01 / SIZE

Mostly small.

Flake sizes follow the exponential spread measured in real snowfall: a great many small flakes and a few large ones. Heavier snow falls as bigger clumps, so turning up the amount makes the flakes larger as well as more numerous.

02 / FALL

About a metre a second.

Snow is light for its size, so each flake soon stops speeding up and falls at a steady speed of about a metre a second, bigger ones a little faster. Gusts sweep across, and neighbouring flakes swirl together instead of each going its own way.

03 / DEPTH

Through a lens.

Only the flakes near the focus distance are sharp. Nearer ones swell into faint discs, and the closest streak over the shutter's short exposure. Far ones keep their light as they shrink, so the distance glitters instead of fading out.
